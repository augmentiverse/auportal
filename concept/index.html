What is the Augmentiverse?

The Augmentiverse is a conceptual framework for a persistent digital layer associated with physical places, built around augmented reality and interoperable standards. It asks how spatial experiences can remain discoverable and useful beyond a single device, application or session.

Noureddine Elmqaddem’s 2026 iJET article develops this AR-first, standards-aligned trajectory. The framework is a research proposition; its publication does not mean that a universal spatial layer already exists.

  • The original research article↗doi.org

Three defining commitments

01
AR-first

Physical reality, first.

Digital experiences enrich the world we inhabit. Physical places, people and objects remain the foundation.

02
Interoperability

Open by architecture.

Shared standards connect devices, content and identity. The aim is a spatial layer that can cross platform boundaries.

03
Spatial persistence

Presence that persists.

Digital content stays associated with a place, across sessions and users. Persistence is a design commitment, not an automatic property of AR.

The reality–virtuality continuum

From a physical place to a shared experience

01

Physical world

Places, people, objects and observations.

02

Spatial reference

Sensing, mapping, localisation and anchors.

03

Interoperable services

Content, runtimes, identity and discovery.

04

Shared experiences

Accessible interactions and governed persistence.

This is an explanatory architecture, not a ratified Augmentiverse protocol. Standards address different layers, and their combination still requires engineering and governance.

Persistence needs stewardship

An object that stays in place while a user walks around is not necessarily persistent across sessions. Persistence requires saved references, repeatable localisation, access policies and a service that keeps content discoverable. Shared presence also requires compatible coordinate frames and synchronisation.

Physical places change. Spatial content needs a lifecycle: provenance, revision, expiry, removal and responsibility for maintenance. Open formats alone do not settle these questions.

Ubiquitous and spatial computing

Weiser’s ubiquitous computing situates computation in everyday environments. Greenwold’s spatial computing connects interactions to representations of real objects and spaces. These are intellectual foundations for understanding the framework; neither source establishes the Augmentiverse as an implemented universal system.
